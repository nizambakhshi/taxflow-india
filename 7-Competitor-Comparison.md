## 7. Where TaxFlow leads

1. **AI that never uploads the document.** Bill OCR and natural-language reporting both run in the browser. Zoho, ClearTax and Refrens have OCR, but as a server feature or a paid add-on; none of the pages fetched promise that the bill never leaves the device.
2. **GSTR-9 / 9C workpaper depth** — books-vs-filed-3B reconciliation, Rule 42/43 reversals, the 180-day credit watch, and an explicit "portal-only data" gap instead of invented numbers. This is CA-suite territory; the small-biz apps stop at GSTR-1/3B.
3. **GSTR-4 / CMP-08 composition workpaper** — line-level HSN/SAC slab split, the ten Notification 17/2020 states, the service provider's 10%-and-₹50L goods cap, the 2.5% deemed debit. No competitor page fetched advertises composition depth at all.
4. **TDS / 194Q workpaper read out of the purchase books** — section table, whole-vs-excess thresholds, deduction on the earlier of credit or payment, 26Q late fee. The billing apps don't do it; the ERP-class tools do it inside a module you pay several times more for.
5. **Bank matching without a paid bank feed** — any statement layout pasted or dropped in, each line scored against documents with the score explained. Zoho, Tally, Busy, Marg and ClearTax all do reconciliation; none advertise working from an arbitrary pasted statement with no feed.
6. **Craft-level things nobody advertises**: ⌘K palette over pages *and* live records — now including the local order book *and*, since **R3-11** (Rule 48 / ADR-045), the two Round-3 registers that existed but could not be reached from anywhere: the filings register (each recorded return period and each open amendment) and the export refund track (each shipping bill and zero-rated file) search synchronously, in the same groups as everything else, a hit landing on the exact tab and `?q=` the page already honors — and every row printing only the engine's own labels, never a due date recomputed or a rupee re-read here — offline invoices that replay through the real save path with a real sequence number, party ageing, festival-weighted targets judged on a prorated pace, an (s, S) reorder planner, a FEFO batch register whose allocator names the expired lot it would draw — before anything is written, a serial register whose warranty window is recomputed from the latest sale (a resale restarts it; a return never forks the unit's history), and a customer statement link that carries the entire statement inside the URL's fragment itself — nothing stored, nothing to revoke, and the numbers identical to the ledger's by construction. Recurring billing goes the other way from the cloud habit: instead of a server cron that silently posts invoices at midnight, the schedule *accrues* — nothing is stored about what is due, the calendar is recomputed from the rule each time you open the page, and a back-dated catch-up bill lands in the financial year it belonged to, which is the GST-correct thing a monthly auto-invoice usually is not. Multi-currency goes the same direction: rather than rewriting the statutory invoice, `/fx` keeps the rupee books untouched and shows the three rupee numbers a foreign bill actually has — the rate you used, the rate your own totals imply, and today's rate — with the gap between them called out as the finding, and a rule-45 date-and-source stamp on every rate rather than a single live conversion factor. Cost centres go the same direction a third time: the tag stores a *percentage* against a document's own id and re-reads the rupees from the books on every load, so a split cannot rot when the bill is amended; the unassigned remainder is computed by subtraction and printed, because a division of the business that loses rupees is worse than no division at all; and a centre is never treated as a legal person — the cross-state branch raises a rule 41A question with a link to the return, not a tax figure invented beside it.
7. **Job work with the clock attached, and a calendar that reads the register instead of asking.** Section 143 is a deadline, not a checkbox: goods sent to a job worker must come back within 12 months (inputs) or 36 (capital goods), and rule 45 treats the *original send date* as the day a supply happened if they do not — so the exposure reaches backwards across periods instead of starting from the day you notice. `/gst/job-work` models exactly that: a lot may stipulate its own period but the engine takes the **shorter** of stipulated and statutory; moulds/dies/tools/templates are a first-class category with **no return period** (`no_deadline`, rather than an invented deadline); a late return is counted *and* reported instead of folding into "settled"; an onward move to a second job worker keeps the original clock; and there is deliberately no "written off" disposition, because leaving units outstanding *is* that statement and a second way to say it only lets the two drift. ITC-04 grids A/B/C/D and the HSN-wise summary are built from the movements inside the chosen period, with the outstanding column as the position at period close across every earlier lot. The compliance calendar has no "sends job work" toggle — `jobWorkEvents()` emits ITC-04 rows only when the register has movements, and the half-yearly-vs-annual dates come from declared turnover, so the calendar and the workpaper cannot disagree. None of the nine vendors fetched states ITC-04 on its pages (per the legend that means *not advertised on the pages fetched*, not that the vendor lacks it); the row's only ✔ is ClearTax, whose page describes job work as a feature rather than as a deadline with a deemed-supply penalty.
8. **A Tally bridge that shows its work before it writes anything.** Seven of the nine vendors advertise moving Tally data, and for a shop with years of vouchers in TallyPrime that bridge *is* the purchase decision — but the bridge is usually a black box: the file is uploaded, a mapping runs on someone's server, and the ledger changes. `/tally` (`src/lib/tally.ts`, **ADR-029**) keeps the file in the browser and separates *reading* from *writing*: `readTally()` parses a tolerant XML dump — Tally's own writer, integration dumps and hand-edited requests differ in case, in whether a value sits in an attribute or a child tag, in whether the file ends where the last tag closes — decides what each object **is** here (a Sales voucher with a Sundry Debtor is an invoice; a ledger under `Sundry Creditor` is a vendor; a Tally group is not a customer, and the reason it is skipped is printed beside it), and `buildPlan()` returns one row per object with its status — `ready` / `blocked` / `skipped` / `already` — the rupees it would write, **why not** for anything it cannot write, and **how it was worked out** wherever a figure had to be derived ("₹tax ÷ ₹taxable", labelled as arithmetic). A file is evidence, not instruction: where it disagrees with itself — ledger lines that do not sum to the printed total, a voucher that does not balance to zero — the disagreement is reported and the file's own lines win, because the lines are what the shop billed. Where an input cannot be resolved the row **blocks rather than guesses**: no party invented, no product created implicitly by a line, no tax rate spread across lines the file cannot attribute it to. Two numbers stay the owner's, not the file's: `invoice_number` comes from `invoice_sequences` and the Tally voucher travels in `notes` (`Tally Sales #V-101 · ref PO-118`) so the two books can always be tied back, and a purchase bill's `bill_number` stays the vendor's own reference because that is what GSTR-2B matches. And **an import never writes money** — a receipt ledger inside a voucher is reported as ₹X already collected and left for `/payments`, the one writer of money. Ticking is the only instruction; `writeOrder()` puts masters before documents so an invoice can find its party, and every row goes through `createInvoice` / `createPurchaseBill` — real sequence number, stock movements, FEFO — never a direct insert. Since R2-27 the same discipline covers the reversal documents: a credit or debit note voucher becomes a note row against the document it *names* — which must sit on the register or be a ready invoice/bill planned from the same file, never invented — and lands through `createCreditNote` / `createDebitNote`, the same creators the two note pages write with. Since **R3-8** (Rule 45 / ADR-042) it covers the two vouchers the bridge used to list as skipped: a **Journal voucher** plans its legs only onto heads that already stand on the journal register and lands as a *draft* through that register's own `writeEntry` — which asks the period seal and burns no number, because the JR number is taken at posting — and a **Delivery Challan voucher** raises a *challan draft* through orderflow's own `appendDraft`, so the number comes from that series' high-water mark in the shop's own series shape. A draft is the whole concession: the import may hand a register a row a human confirms, and may not make a posting, mint a number, or create a head, a party, a product, or money — cash and bank legs in a journal block, an unbalanced voucher blocks rather than being squared, tax inside a challan is reported while the untaxed lines win, and an unknown movement reason defaults to `other` with a read-before-confirm flag. Run backwards the same discipline: our rows become the XML envelope TallyPrime imports with `Create`/`Alter` as the only actions, the tax ledgers a voucher names written beside it (`Output IGST @18%` into a company that has no such ledger is the classic failed import), and the rupees being handed over counted and printed before the file is downloaded.

9. **A project register that measures work and refuses to own the money.** Six of the nine vendors show `·` for project or time tracking and only Zoho states it, at the Professional tier — which usually means a project is a *cost bucket* the accounting engine already has. `/projects` (`src/lib/projects.ts`, **ADR-030**) is built the other way round: the register stores **measurements and nothing else** — hours, and documents attached by id — so every rupee on the money side is re-read from the books on each load through the same `bookDocs()` the cost-centre register uses. That is what keeps a project's numbers honest to the invoice the tax papers were filed from: an invoice amended in Postgres changes what this page says about the job without anyone re-keying it, and the register physically cannot hold a stale amount because it never holds one. The second refusal is the one the competition blurs: an hour here is **priced — and, since R2-36, costed only where the row names whose hour it was**. A rate on an hour is what it would be billed at, never what it cost to do — contribution is therefore billed revenue less attached spend, with the labour value *and* the costed hour sitting *beside* it rather than inside it. A margin that subtracted your own billing rate from your own revenue would be double counting with extra steps. (Since R2-31 the app *does* know what an hour costs — `/payroll`'s `costPerHour()` / `blendedHourCost()` divide a month's employer cost by that person's paid days and their own hours a day — and since R2-36 a timesheet row can **name the person those hours were**: `/projects` costs that row at that person's own entry-month — the page hands the register's own `HourCostRow`s to `costHours()`, still importing no payroll code — and prints the figure as *after named hours* **beside** contribution, marked *partial* until every hour is named, a refusal keeping the register's own sentence, so contribution is still billed revenue less attached spend and an hour nobody names stays priced only.) R2-37 then carried the same discipline one step further: the timesheet can **draw** its hours from the payroll register's kept day book — a person-month of marks read through that register's own `markWork()` and handed to the projects library as data, present days proposed at the register's own hours a day, half a mark proposed as half, every other mark refused with the register's own sentence, a day already on the project un-tickable — and only the shop's tick writes, through the page's own entry validator, the drawn row naming its person so it is costed the moment it lands. The third is the shape of the obligation: the thing a shop with services actually loses is not a cost allocation, it is **delivered work that never became paper**. `unbilledGroups()` buckets unbilled hours per project per calendar month — the supply date of a service is the day the work was given, so the month is the unit rule 47 speaks about — and prints the earliest supply in the bucket, the date the invoice was due, the days left or days over, and a line you can paste at `/invoices/new`. Marking hours billed writes a **reference stamp, not an invoice**: it never mints a document number and never touches money, because `createInvoice` is the only writer of invoices and `/payments` the only writer of money; the page says so, and a reference that matches no invoice in the books becomes a finding rather than a silence. And the statutory claim stays the size the source supports: `SERVICE_INVOICE_DAYS = 30` is an exported, cited constant — CBIC's *Handbook on Invoicing under GST* (17-06-2025, §2.1.2), section 31(2) read with rule 47 — with a named link beside it, because notifications widen the window for classes of supplier and only gst.gov.in can settle which applies to you. A late row here is a date, a rule and a link to the 3B workpaper, never a tax figure.

10. **A recipe book that answers *"what cannot I make today?"* before it answers *"what did it cost?"*.** Three of the nine vendors advertise manufacturing (Busy, ClearTax, Refrens) and the ERP-class ones sell it as a module priced for a factory, but every one of them starts from the bill of materials as a *costing* document — which is the wrong question first, because a shop with shelves knows what it can build before it knows what the build costs. `/inventory/recipes` (`src/lib/bom.ts`, **ADR-031**) flips the order. A recipe stores **quantities and no money at all**: unit cost is read from `products.purchase_price` on every load, so correcting a price on the product master re-values every recipe that uses it, and a component with no price recorded yields no figure rather than the ₹0 a stored snapshot would have frozen in. Its first row is a cost, but it is labelled **`Material cost / unit`** — a reading with its source in its name, printing `— (2 unpriced)` rather than ₹0 when the catalogue has no price — and immediately under it sits **`Can make now`**, how many units the shelf would cover today, with the limiting part printed beside it as *held up by …*; the **materials board** then asks that same question of the order book, folding confirmed sales orders into what is still to make, so the register tells you what to order rather than what you already knew. The **build** is where the discipline shows: it records what was physically drawn, and stock moves only through `record_stock_movement` on the existing `adjustment_*` types — same RPC as a manual adjustment, same audit trail, no new table under ADR-013 — while the page refuses an over-draw before it writes and **reverses** the movements when a build is cancelled, because the library's `cancelBuild()` only flips a status and would otherwise leave rows claiming stock had not moved. And a build is **not a supply**: making your own goods for your own GSTIN has no recipient, so nothing here reaches GSTR-1 — the same line `/gst/job-work` draws from the other side (the customer's goods, our process, section 143's clock). The limits are printed rather than hidden: the work-in-progress posting that was one of them since R2-28 **shipped at R3-17** (`src/lib/wip.ts` + **Rule 54 / ADR-051**) — a built run's pair of journal drafts lands through `/journal`'s own `writeEntry`, the shop names every head and the engine invents neither a rupee nor a head; what still stands as a limit is no cost layer behind `purchase_price`, and no back-flush from a sale — while a run that is recorded does say **which lot it emptied** a run that is recorded does say **which lot it emptied** and lands the finished units as a lot of its own (R2-29). Since R2-30 the board also stops being the end of the conversation: `mergedShortages()` folds every plan's shortfalls into one row per component — the *same* fold the stat card reads, which is what makes the figure on the screen and the quantity on the paper one number rather than two — and `shoppingPlan()` arranges those rows under the vendor each part last came in from, at the rate that bill paid, or the catalogue's price when no bill names one, each line labelled with which basis it is standing on. A vendor it will not write to is printed with the reason rather than dropped (no bill ever named a supplier; the supplier has since left the vendor list; the card carries no state, so there is no place of supply to write — the same refusal the order register would have made on its own page), and an order with one unpriced line is given **no value at all** rather than the sum of the lines that happen to have prices. What is raised then goes through `raiseDrafts()` in `src/lib/orderflow.ts`, the purchase-order register's own high-water numbering, so a draft born on a manufacturing page is indistinguishable from one typed at `/purchase-orders` — and stays a draft, because a purchase order is a promise to a vendor and this app does not make those on your behalf.

11. **A backup the user owns, restored through the pages that own the data.** ADR-013's device-side registers were a single point of failure no cloud competitor has an equivalent of: their books live on their servers, ours lived in one browser. `/vault` (`src/lib/vault.ts`, **ADR-033**) writes all thirty-five of them into one `taxflow-vault-<org>-<date>.json` — walking a named list of register specs rather than sweeping localStorage, because the sweep would catch the Supabase auth keys (`sb-*`) beside the books. The restore side is where the discipline lives: every register goes back **through its own `write` path** (never a direct `setItem`, which would bypass every parser and version check), is then **read back through its own loader**, and the plan prints `from → seen` per register so rows a parser dropped are counted, not lost. Per-block FNV-1a checksums refuse a truncated or hand-edited file *by name* even when it still parses; the nine-state plan (`new` / `replace` / `blank` / `same` / `foreign` / …) is the page's main product and comes before any write, with `blank` selectable but never pre-ticked — two empty registers and a wiped one look identical from outside, and that is the user's call; the five evidence logs (offline outbox, e-way bills, the Tally import, palette recents, the compliance profile) ride in the file but are **never written back**, because putting a pending invoice into the outbox would replay a sync and bill the same sale twice. And the vault's own log is deliberately not in the vault: a backup that recorded its backups would grow every time it was restored, and "when did you last take this file" belongs to the browser that took it. **R3-9 deepened the row by making that log keep a fingerprint per register** — each recorded backup stamps `{rows, checksum}` (the same FNV-1a the file's block carries) on the event, and `registerFreshness()` compares what this browser holds now, checksum against checksum, to answer *which books moved since your last file* in seven states. No competitor page fetched says anything about the state of a *device-side* backup after it left, because none of them has one: the claim this row now supports is that the app can say, without opening the file, what a wiped browser would take with it — and can say *cannot tell* when its memory does not go back far enough. The fingerprints stay on the device, so a second laptop restoring the same file inherits the books, not the claim.

12. **A salary register that computes the year instead of a percentage of the month.** Three of the eight competitor columns say anything about payroll — Tally at ✔ (₹750 + 18% GST a month), Zoho at ~ (its Premium tier) and ClearTax at ✔ — and the other five show `·`, which by the legend means *not stated on the page fetched*, not *absent from the product*. But all three sell payroll as **attendance software plus a bank file**, which is why none of those pages mentions section 192. R2-32 took the attendance half of that sentence — the month run reads a pasted sheet (`planAttendance()` in the same library) and writes one lost-day figure per person through the month form's own validator, refusing to guess the direction of a bare number, to arbitrate between two day columns that disagree, or to zero somebody the sheet forgot — and R2-34 then kept the *days*: the register's own day book holds one attendance mark per person per day (tapped, or bridged from a page ruled with dates; a refused line is carried by its machine-readable kind, so a mark it cannot read stops the whole page while a row with no marks is simply noted), pure evidence that holds no rupee until one deliberate pull counts each person's lost days through that same validator — a person the book never wrote is left untouched rather than zeroed, and nobody is marked present by default. Their product keeps the roster and the device feed; ours keeps a book that says exactly what was written and names what it disagrees with — no biometrics, no shift timings, no overtime priced off the marks, nothing filed from it — because the register's job is the salary, not the gate. Since R2-37 the same book also feeds the jobs: `/projects` draws its hours from the marks — a present day proposed at this register's own `hours_per_day`, half a mark as half, every other mark refused with this register's own sentence, only the shop's tick writing, and only through the projects page's own validator — and the drawn row names the person, so it is costed the moment it lands; the book itself is untouched by the draw. `/payroll` (`src/lib/payroll.ts`, **ADR-034**) builds the half an Indian small business actually carries in its head. It stores **structure and exceptions**: six monthly components with their PF- and ESI-carrying flags, an age band, a state, joining and leaving dates, hours a day, a per-employee professional-tax figure, and four numbers per month (lost days, arrears, bonus, recovery). It stores **no gross, no PF, no TDS and no net pay** — every rupee is recomputed on load, for the same reason the recipe book and the cost centres refuse to store money: a stored figure disagrees with the structure the moment anyone corrects a component. The tax is the part nobody else does in a browser: section 192 is a *method*, so `taxWalk()` runs April forward, adds the elapsed months' actual wages, projects the rest at the current month's rate, sheds the standard deduction and the **year's** professional tax under section 16(iii) (`min(₹2,500, monthly × the months actually on the rolls)`), computes the year's tax once — bands, 87A with its marginal relief, the surcharge cliffs measured from the foot of the band, cess — and divides the residue by the months still to come, so a June bonus lifts July onward and the March row absorbs whatever the estimate under-collected. That is the only construction in which twelve instalments add up to the year, and it is why nothing is stored as "TDS deducted". The rates know their own birthdays: the PF wage ceiling **moved from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 on 17-09-2026** (S.O. 5109(E)) and a run dated before that day is computed on the old ceiling and prints which one it used — while the ESI limit stayed at ₹21,000, and the two statutes disagreeing on the same slip is printed as a finding rather than smoothed away. Absence and zero are never the same row (`pt: number | null`: a dash and "not recorded" against a real ₹0 that claims it); an employee aged 60 or above is **refused** a tax figure rather than handed the general schedule; a financial year the tables do not hold returns nothing rather than an extrapolation; and the unsettled questions stay open on the page — whether the *pensionable* salary for EPS moved with the September ceiling is the owner's setting, printed on every slip, and professional tax has **no national due date to print**, so its row sits in the calendar's *State dates* rail and stays out of the `.ics` rather than inventing a start date. The calendar, in turn, **stops guessing**: it retires the filing profile's monthly PF/PT tick-box rows for any month the salary books have actually been run, and shows the proved row with the deposit date and the rupees the books hold. And because there was no payroll, `/projects` long had to say an hour is *priced, not costed* (item 9); `costPerHour()` and `blendedHourCost()` compute the employer-cost figure and `/payroll` prints it on its own Cost tab, and since R2-36 a timesheet row can name the person those hours were — `/projects` reads the figure **as data** (`HourCostRow`s handed to `costHours()`, still no import of `payroll.ts`) and prints it as *after named hours* beside contribution, never inside it — while the hours nobody names stay priced only. Nothing here pays anyone: the challans are settled on the EPFO, ESIC and income-tax portals, and `/expenses` and `/payments` remain the only writers of money in this app. **R2-33 printed the sheet the employee keeps** (`src/lib/payslip.ts`, laid out by `/payroll/payslip`) — and printed it as a *reading*: every rupee on a slip is the engine's own figure, the suite polices the tie rather than the layout (`earnings_total = monthly + arrears + bonus`, `deduction_total = loss of pay + statutory legs`, `employer_cost − net_pay = employer legs + deductions − loss of pay`), the line says *Net payable* with its four boundaries beside it (**this is not Form 16**), the four statutory codes a salary document is asked to carry print a dash and name the gap rather than invent a value, a refused month produces no figures at all, and the month packs one A4 sheet per person into a single PDF. The paper half of every payroll product's offer is now ours; the *issuing* half is deliberately not — nothing is emailed, acknowledged or signed, the file leaves the browser as a download (ADR-034 amended again).
